# Settings

> [简体中文](SETTINGS.md)

What each feature does is in [Features](FEATURES.en.md).

The settings page appears both in the settings dialog (the "Claude Style" tab) and on the plugin page, in seven tabs:

| Tab | Settings |
|---|---|
| General | Theme style, Username, Animation, Open popovers on hover |
| Appearance | Colours, Typefaces, Home layout, Mascot, Where it appears, Brand name, Brand mark, Wordmark, Image colours, Accent colour |
| Composer | Composer restyle, Redraw the model picker (with Quick providers and the Peak rate meter under it), Redraw the permission control |
| Sidebar | Collapse the sidebar settings area (with the Account-hold easter egg under it: Off / 中文 / English), Sidebar search, Search box style, In progress / Archived view, Float docked panels, Sidebar layout (Relaxed / Compact) |
| Conversation | Conversation navigator, Chat-area animations, Conversation statistics position (Inside the context popover / On its own line), Composer caret motion, Chat / Trajectory tabs |
| Desktop | Header in the title bar |
| Motion | Waiting-line animation (Highlight sweep / Colour wash) |

Every feature that takes over part of the host's interface has its own switch; turning it off brings the host's original back at once, without a reload. The conversation area's effects — the follow, the automatic folding with its rolling door, the text fade, the file change rows, the send flight, the counted figures on each process group's header and the line saying what the model is doing — share the one Chat-area animations switch: on plays this plugin's set, off hands the conversation back to the host with the host's own words; the caret keeps its own three-way choice.

A few settings only work while another one is on; while that one is off they grey out and take no input, still showing the value you set, and a line under the description says what they wait for. Redraw the model picker (with Quick providers and the Peak rate meter) and Redraw the permission control are drawn into the restyled composer, so they grey out while Composer restyle is Off; Conversation statistics position only works while the restyle covers the conversation (Conversation only or All); the Account-hold easter egg opens from the account popover, so it greys out with Collapse the sidebar settings area.

**Desktop**: the settings on this tab act on the Windows desktop window only. Opened in a browser or in the macOS window, the tab starts with a line saying so; a change is saved all the same and shows in the Windows window.

**Account-hold easter egg**: the account row at the top of the account popover opens a full-page replica of Claude's account-hold page, in the language chosen on the Sidebar tab whatever the interface language. With Off chosen the account row is a plain name and pressing it opens nothing.

In the settings dialog, this page's heading row ends with a GitHub mark; pressing it opens the plugin's repository in a new tab.

**Opened from a remote address**: DSH saves settings only from a page opened at a local address such as localhost. A page reached through a LAN address or a tunnel (a phone, another computer) shows the look and the settings DSH holds, and the settings page opens with a line saying so, its controls disabled. Settings changed on the computer running DSH reach the remote page without a reload.

**Custom brand**: the last rows of the Appearance tab put your own brand over the one Theme style picks. Brand name replaces the brand text at the top of the sidebar; Brand mark replaces the mark at the top of the sidebar, on the collapsed rail, on the new-conversation page and as the account row's fallback picture; Wordmark replaces the name beside the mark, its width following the image's proportions at the row's height, and while it is in place the brand name is not shown. The images take PNG, SVG and WebP up to 2 MB each and are kept under `dsh-claude-style/brand/` in the DSH home directory; dropping `mark.png` (or `.svg`, `.webp`) and `wordmark.png` into that directory by hand works too, after a page reload. Image colours set to Original shows the images in their own colours; Text colour uses each image as a shape filled with the text colour where it stands, which suits a one-colour mark and reads in both light and dark. Accent colour recolours buttons, selected marks, links and focus rings, with the text on a button picked dark or light to read on it; unset, the chosen brand's accent stays, and while Colours is set to Follow the host the accent has no effect. Clearing or removing any of them puts the chosen brand's own back in its place.

**Peak rate meter**: every row of the model menu carries the rate in force for that model right now. Peak, off-peak and a dated promotion each get their own mark, the colour separates expensive from cheap, and the multiplier is followed by how long it lasts; hovering the badge prints the whole judgement and what it becomes. The judgement is made from each provider's own published schedule in that provider's own time zone: outside the weekday windows, and on weekends and public holidays in full, the rate is off-peak, and a window crossing midnight belongs to the day it starts. A model with no schedule published carries no badge. The schedules are one snapshot in the public [offpeakclock.com](https://offpeakclock.com) catalog: the host half — not the page — fetches it, keeps a copy on disk and refreshes it every 24 hours, and judges by the copy shipped in the package when it cannot be reached.

**Sidebar search**: with Sidebar search on, the sidebar carries a search box whose palette finds sessions (titles and content), projects, plugins, Skills and shortcuts; picking a content hit lands the page on the turn holding that message, paging that turn's records in first on a long history. Search box style places it: Cover the brand (the default) puts it in the brand row, standing over the brand while the pointer is anywhere over the sidebar and giving the brand back when the pointer leaves; Standalone gives it a row of its own under the brand row, shown always; Icon draws no box of the plugin's, keeping DSH's own search button where the host draws it and taking only its click over, so pressing it opens this plugin's palette. The content match reads your own messages and the model's answers alone: injected context, the Skill catalog and reasoning never reach the results.

**Sidebar layout**: Relaxed (the default) is how the sidebar looks now; Compact takes the spacing down alone — the gap between the top rows (New session, Plugins, Automation tasks, and the search box's own row when it stands alone) and the workspace, the workspace heading's own margins, the conversation rows' height and spacing, the distance between two sections, and the account area's padding and row margins. Control shapes, type sizes, icons and colours are untouched, so both layouts hold the same things to press.

**Alongside other theme plugins**: with Colours and Typefaces set to Follow the host, the skin no longer rewrites the host's colours and fonts and keeps only its layout and controls; the colours are left to DSH itself, or to another theme plugin enabled at the same time. With [dsh-wallpaper-engine](https://github.com/elysia395/dsh-wallpaper-engine), for example, the wallpaper shows through the sidebar and the conversation, and the skin's own popovers take that plugin's glass, translucent and blurring the picture behind them. **Alongside [dsh-chat-ux](https://github.com/alm-allen/dsh-chat-ux)**: that plugin implements the same chat-area interactions (the rolling door, automatic folding, the token fade, file change rows, the send flight, the drawn caret), and two copies of them intercept each other's clicks and press the same controls. So this skin stands down when it sees that plugin: the Chat-area animations and Composer caret motion rows on the settings page's Conversation tab have their controls disabled while each still shows the value you set, with a line underneath in the accent colour reading "Managed by dsh-chat-ux". Your settings are not rewritten, and they take effect as they are once dsh-chat-ux is removed.

**Chat-area follow**: at the structural moments — a thinking row folding, a tool call row arriving — a reader sitting at the bottom is handed back to the host's own follow, instead of being left tens of pixels short by the burst of content; a process group capped in the Standard and Compact tiers (thinking and tool output kept in one scrolling body) is caught up the same way. Catching up runs on a curve: a trickle of characters settles softly and a burst glides at one steady speed before landing. While content streams, the main scroller's own follow is walked along that curve too rather than written to the end in a single frame — with a fast stream the newest lines trail just below the fold and slide into place once it stops. A message you send does not go through it: the host's scroll to bring it into view lands at once. Once the reader scrolls away from the bottom himself, the plugin stays out of it until he returns.

**Automatic folding**: a thinking row opens while the model reasons and folds back when it stops; a running process group opens and folds back once that piece of work ends. A row or group the reader pressed himself keeps what he chose for that phase.

**The reasoning's window**: the open reasoning stands in a window that grows with it: the window is as tall as the text until it reaches the height reasoning may take (the host's own cap on a process group's body), and there it stays while the text steps up two lines every 0.84 s (half a second of travel) behind a softened edge at each side, stopping at the newest line. Below that cap nothing is faded and nothing slides — the reader follows the reasoning as it is written — and the conversation is only pushed down over the stretch up to the cap. Once the reasoning stops the window comes off, and the reader who opens the row reads the whole text; with Animation set to Reduced the window is never set and the row lies open as the host drew it.

**Counted process headers**: a process group's header says what the group holds, in place of the host's list of activities — "2 thoughts, 1 tool call". Empty figures are left out, each number rolls as the work grows, and the whole line sweeps the way the host's words do while that piece of work runs. A header without the room for the sentence (a narrow window, a narrow row) falls back to the compact figures, `Thinking×2 · Tools×1`, and takes the sentence back when the room returns; the control's accessible name is the sentence either way.

**The line saying what the model is doing**: while a turn runs, the line at the end of the conversation is no longer a fixed "Deep diving for N s ···": it names what that turn is doing now — "Thinking…", "Preparing a tool call…", "Running tools…", "Writing…" — with the time the turn has taken beside it in the host's own units. Two of those states say something more: a call in flight names the file it works on ("Reading app.js", "Editing notes.md", "Writing out.txt" — the file's own name, cut past 32 characters), and reasoning carrying an English present-continuous clause hands the line the model's own words ("Checking the file is true": from the participle to that clause's end, cut past 40 characters, a negated clause and reasoning in another language passed over, and "Thinking…" where there is none). The line follows the host's own publishes, so a call is read the moment it starts running; every wording written is locked for a second, and the pass that finds the lock run out writes the newest state of that moment — whatever came and went inside the second is passed over, so a turn that moves quickly still changes the line at most once a second. A call's own words are the one thing the lock does not pass over: a small file read inside the second is written as soon as the lock ends, ahead of the state of that moment. A wording that changes runs transitions.dev's "Text states swap": the old one blurs and fades upward, the new one rises from below (about 0.15 s), and the box takes the arriving wording's width in one move, so the clock beside it travels to its new place (about 0.24 s) rather than jumping twice. Where no state can be read at all it falls back to the host's own "Deep diving", and once the model has been silent for ten seconds "No response yet" appears beside the clock. The line sweeps, and the host's whale and its screen-reader announcement stay; wording wider than the room this line has is cut with an ellipsis while the clock and the badge keep their places; with Animation set to Reduced the wording is written at once, with none of those three phases. As long as the conversation follows its tail the line rests above the composer, so content arriving below no longer drags it around; scrolling back yourself leaves it travelling with the content.

**Send flight**: on submission the composer card lifts as it is and narrows into the bubble as it travels, its words re-flowing into the shape, landing on the message row: the real bubble shows underneath and the flying copy fades out, so the words sharpen into place.

**File change rows**: a write or edit dispatched from inside a run_code program carries the `+n -m` tail and an expandable diff card, and its path opens the file; a failed or interrupted row keeps its verdict.

**New text fades in**: characters arriving in a streaming answer start faint and settle over about 0.12 s, staggered slightly by arrival order; a block arriving whole, a burst of thousands of characters and text that just reflowed from a fold stay solid.

**A rolling door for folds**: opening or closing a row (a tool card, a thinking row, a command card) or a process group moves the height frame by frame, really pushing the content below away or pulling it back. The door only rolls the stretch the reader can see, so any length moves at the same speed, and a body holding several cards rolls as one door. It switches with Chat-area animations, together with the entrance fade of an expanded body.

**Composer caret motion**: the composer's text caret is drawn by the plugin and glides when it moves; a question card's answer box and a queued message's inline editor are covered as well. The Conversation tab offers Every move (the default), Explicit moves and Off.

**Waiting-line animation**: the Motion tab's one setting, and it is about the words on the line that says what the model is doing: neither choice carries text, each card being that line's own words, moving, all the time. Inside a card the words take turns between two wordings — "Thinking…" and "Deep diving" — the old one leaving upward and the new one rising back in, which is the swap the line itself runs, over either paint: Highlight sweep (the default) crosses a band of light over the glyphs at the rhythm of the host's own rows, and Colour wash makes the letters themselves move through colour — seven radial washes over a grey ramp, orbiting the glyph box while the hue turns a full circle. One press on a card changes it at once; on the line itself the wording, its swap and the clock beside it are the same either way; with Animation set to Reduced the preview stands on one wording and the line holds its still frame.

**Conversation statistics position**: the session's eight figures have two places to live. Inside the context popover (the default) puts them in the panel that ring opens and leaves only the ring under the input card; On its own line writes the turns and steps, the output speed, the tokens read in, the tokens written back and the cache-hit share on a line of their own below the input card, with the ring itself at the end. A figure on that line re-enters character by character whenever it moves (every step, and all through a streaming answer), and the cache share and the ring each take their own colour band: the share runs from red in the nineties to dark green at 99%, the occupancy from green at a fifth to red at two fifths. A window too narrow for the whole line makes it give way one step at a time: first the cache share's words, then the turns and steps, then the number beside the ring (the ring stays), then the tokens read in and written back as one total, and last the counts folded into k / M / B — and the same order gives them back when the window grows.

**Conversation navigator**: the column of short marks at the conversation's right edge holds one per turn and follows where you are reading. Reach it with the pointer and it opens into a list: each row carries the message that started its turn and sits exactly where that turn's mark was — the turn you are reading stays in place, and the turn under the pointer is still under it. The wheel scrolls the list and a click glides the page to the turn, loading earlier history first when it is not loaded yet; the Chat / Trajectory tabs at the top of the conversation stay up while the pointer is on the list. Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ jump to the previous or the next turn, and held or repeated they keep stepping; while the composer holds a draft the keys stay with it. The turn you land on flashes a short line over its start. With [dsh-plugin-msg-nav](https://github.com/SherUnlocked-4869/dsh-plugin-msg-nav) installed too, Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ stay with that plugin.

**Mascot**: a pixel companion stands on the composer's top edge and changes its animation with what the agent is doing (thinking, writing and calling tools, several sessions at work, subagents, waiting on you, compacting the context, finished, failed, asleep). Follow the brand shows the pixel crab under Claude and Deepy the whale under DeepSeek; either can be picked for good, or none. Where it appears keeps it to the new-conversation page, or puts it in conversations as well.

# Features

> [简体中文](FEATURES.md)

What each feature does and where its switch lives; the six tabs, their rows and what each tier changes are in [Settings](SETTINGS.en.md).

- **Custom brand**: Puts your own brand name, mark and wordmark over the chosen brand: the top of the sidebar, the collapsed rail, the new-conversation page and the account row's fallback picture all take them, with the images in their own colours or inked in the text colour. An accent colour of your own can recolour buttons, selected marks, links and focus rings.
- **Composer**: The input card on the new-conversation page and in conversations is redrawn after Claude Code Desktop: attachments sit in one row of tiles, the context usage is a ring in the toolbar, a press anywhere on the card starts typing, and a conversation resting at the bottom moves up with the card as it grows; an empty composer shows Claude's own hint. The skin's popover cards — the model, reasoning, permission and account cards and the new-conversation page's two menus — grow out of their buttons from a little smaller and close faster, shrinking only slightly as they fade. The Composer tab chooses the home page alone, the conversation alone, both, or neither.
- **Home layout**: Classic is the centred greeting over the input card. Studio moves the greeting to the top left, pins the input card to the window's bottom edge and fills the space between with a usage panel: a heat map of recent usage and the usage and cost of each model. The new-conversation page greets you by name with a line that changes with the time of day, and a different one each time the page comes back. In both layouts, the home page opening raises the brand mark and then, a beat behind, the greeting, each out of a short blur; the usage panel draws placeholders for its figures first, and when the figures are ready the placeholders fade while the figures come out of the blur.
- **Mascot**: A pixel companion stands on the composer's top edge and changes its animation with what the agent is doing (thinking, writing and calling tools, several sessions at work, subagents, waiting on you, compacting the context, finished, failed, asleep). Follow the brand shows the pixel crab under Claude and Deepy the whale under DeepSeek; either can be picked for good, or none. Where it appears keeps it to the new-conversation page, or puts it in conversations as well.
- **Permission control**: The composer's permission menu becomes a row of segments on the classic home; the studio home and conversations carry one trigger with its tier card, shaped like the composer's other pickers. The presets are the host's own catalog, a plugin's preset included; the new-conversation page shows the one a new session will start in.
- **Session numbers**: The session statistics and the token usage follow the host's data live and stand in the context ring's popover by default: the host's own context rows, followed by this session's detail rows — turns and steps, the output speed, the tokens read in and written back, the cache-hit share. On its own line writes them on a line of their own below the input card with the ring at its end, and pressing that line opens and closes the same popover (opening and closing on hover stays the ring's own path). A number that moves re-enters character by character (the last two a beat behind), no separator glyph stands between the groups — the line's own gap is what sets them apart — and the cache share and the ring's occupancy each take their own colour band.
- **Peak rate meter**: Every row of the model picker carries the rate in force for that model right now: peak, off-peak and a dated promotion each get their own mark, the colour says expensive or cheap, and the countdown says how long it lasts; hovering reads the whole judgement, the state in force and what it becomes and when. Each provider is judged on its own clock, public holidays are off-peak in full, and windows crossing midnight and daylight-saving changes are resolved against real instants.
- **Model picker**: The composer's model menu becomes a two-level card: the first level holds the official service and the quick providers you picked, each model with its vendor mark and a line of description; the second level holds the rest.
- **Effort**: The reasoning effort gets a trigger of its own beside the model, opening a slider. It switches with the model picker, since the host keeps the effort inside its own model menu.
- **Home page directory and preset menus**: The directory and agent-preset menus on the new-conversation page are drawn as the skin's popover cards, parked over their own buttons, one open at a time.
- **Quick providers**: The settings page picks which providers' models the model picker's first level carries; a provider gone from the catalog is marked Removed and can be unchecked.
- **Sidebar account area**: The sidebar footer's settings entry folds into the account popover, which carries the account header, other plugins' footer entries and the settings row; on the desktop the host's own account menu is used.
- **Account-hold easter egg**: The account row at the top of the account popover opens a full-page replica of Claude's account-hold page, growing out of the centre as it fades in. Every control on it, and Esc, closes it — it shrinks back as it fades — and nothing it names actually happens. The Sidebar tab picks its language or turns it off, leaving the account row a plain name.
- **In progress / Archived**: The sidebar's workspace section splits into In progress and Archived; Archived is one flat list whose rows can be restored or deleted.
- **Sidebar search**: A search box in the sidebar opens a palette that finds sessions (titles and content), projects, plugins, Skills and shortcuts, through the host's own dialog and navigation; picking a content hit lands on the turn holding that message rather than at the top of the session. Search box style places it: Cover the brand (the default) keeps it in the brand row, standing over the brand while the pointer is over the sidebar; Standalone gives it a row of its own under the brand row, shown always; Icon leaves the host's own search button in place and takes only its click over.
- **Conversation navigator**: The column of short marks at the conversation's right edge holds one per turn and follows where you are reading. Reach it with the pointer and it opens into a list: each row carries the message that started its turn and sits exactly where that turn's mark was — the turn you are reading stays in place, and the turn under the pointer is still under it. The wheel scrolls the list and a click glides the page to the turn, loading earlier history first when it is not loaded yet; the Chat / Trajectory tabs at the top of the conversation stay up while the pointer is on the list. Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ jump to the previous or the next turn, and held or repeated they keep stepping; while the composer holds a draft the keys stay with it. The turn you land on flashes a short line over its start. With [dsh-plugin-msg-nav](https://github.com/SherUnlocked-4869/dsh-plugin-msg-nav) installed too, Alt+↑ / Alt+↓ stay with that plugin.
- **Chat-area follow**: At the structural moments — a thinking row folding, a tool call row arriving — a reader sitting at the bottom is handed back to the host's own follow, instead of being left tens of pixels short by the burst of content; a process group capped in the Standard and Compact tiers (thinking and tool output kept in one scrolling body) is caught up the same way. Catching up runs on a curve: a trickle of characters settles softly and a burst glides at one steady speed before landing. While content streams, the main scroller's own follow is walked along that curve too rather than written to the end in a single frame — with a fast stream the newest lines trail just below the fold and slide into place once it stops. A message you send does not go through it: the host's scroll to bring it into view lands at once. Once the reader scrolls away from the bottom himself, the plugin stays out of it until he returns.
- **Automatic folding and the rolling door**: A thinking row opens while the model reasons and folds back when it stops; a running process group opens and folds back once that piece of work ends. A row or group the reader pressed himself keeps what he chose for that phase. The open reasoning stands in a window that grows with it: the window is as tall as the text until it reaches the height reasoning may take, and there it stays while the text steps up two lines at a steady beat behind a softened edge at each side, stopping at the newest line; once the reasoning stops the window comes off and the reader who opens the row reads it whole, and with Animation set to Reduced the window is never set. A process group's header says what the group holds — "2 thoughts, 1 tool call", empty figures left out — each number rolling as the work grows, and the whole line sweeps the way the host's words do while that piece of work runs; a header without the room for the sentence falls back to `Thinking×2 · Tools×1` and takes the sentence back when the room returns, with the sentence as the control's name either way. Opening or closing a row (a tool card, a thinking row, a command card) or a process group moves the height frame by frame, really pushing the content below away or pulling it back. The door only rolls the stretch the reader can see, so any length moves at the same speed, and a body holding several cards rolls as one door.
- **The line saying what the model is doing**: While a turn runs, the line at the end of the conversation is no longer a fixed "Deep diving for N s ···": it names what that turn is doing now — "Thinking…", "Preparing a tool call…", "Running tools…", "Writing…" — with the time the turn has taken beside it in the host's own units. Three of those states say something more: a call in flight names the file it works on ("Reading app.js", "Editing notes.md", "Writing out.txt" — the file's own name); a command call declares a description in its arguments (the host requires one saying what the command does), and the line writes it as the model wrote it ("Regenerate the changelog": the first line); and reasoning carrying a continuous clause hands the line the model's own words ("Checking the file is true", and "正在读取 app.js" where the model reasons in Chinese: from the participle to that clause's end, with where a sentence ends left to the page's own sentence rules, so a dot inside a file name or a version number does not cut it; a negated clause, a plan the model has not started and reasoning carrying no clause are passed over, and "Thinking…" where there is none). All three are carried whole: how much of one shows is the width this line itself has, and past that the words are clipped with an ellipsis while the clock and the badge keep their places. The Conversation tab's Waiting-line detail choice set to Simple reads none of these specifics and names the bare states alone; Precise (the default) reads them as written. The line follows the host's own publishes, so a call is read the moment it starts running; every wording written is locked for a second, and the pass that finds the lock run out writes the newest state of that moment — whatever came and went inside the second is passed over, so a turn that moves quickly still changes the line at most once a second. A call's own words are the one thing the lock does not pass over: a small file read inside the second is written as soon as the lock ends, ahead of the state of that moment. The model's own clause has a rule of its own: the stretches between two of them, where the reasoning reads as the bare thinking state or as nothing at all, do not take it off the line — it stands until the next intent arrives, whether that is a newer clause, a call's own words, or a state such as writing; with the Precise status line switched off no clause is read at all, so nothing stands. A wording that changes runs transitions.dev's "Text states swap": the old one blurs and fades upward, the new one rises from below, and the box takes the arriving wording's width in one move, so the clock beside it travels to its new place rather than jumping twice. Where no state can be read at all the line stands on that brand's own word — the same one it says while the model is thinking: the skin's own "Thinking…" under the Claude brand, the host's own "Deep diving" under the DeepSeek brand — and once the model has been silent for ten seconds "No response yet" appears beside the clock, sliding onto the line from below and to the left while it pops out of nothing, and shrinking away as soon as the model speaks. The line sweeps — the Motion tab can give it a colour wash that moves through the letters instead — and the host's whale, which is DeepSeek's own character, gives way under the Claude brand to the skin's own loader: three 4px dots in the accent in force, which bounce the way the snippet has them while the model thinks (each rising 8px and flattening as it leaves the floor, half a second a bounce with the second dot starting two fifths of the period behind the first and the third three fifths) and take a second motion once the turn writes or works — each dot walking its own line, three lines 120° apart with one of them run the other way, the dot largest at either end and smallest as it crosses the middle, 1.25s a round trip with as long spent going out as coming back; the two motions never cut: the lane leaving fades each of its dots out where its own motion next turns back — off the floor for a bounce, off a far end for a walk — and the lane arriving fades each of its dots in where that motion next carries it, each fade over one of its own legs, so no dot is ever moved to a pose its motion does not carry; a motion stands for 2s before the next may take it over, so a state that comes and goes inside that stretch leaves it standing, while under the other brands the host's whale tail stands; the host's screen-reader announcement stays either way; wording wider than the room this line has is cut with an ellipsis while the clock and the badge keep their places; with Animation set to Reduced the wording is written at once, with none of those three phases. As long as the conversation follows its tail the line rests above the composer, so content arriving below no longer drags it around; scrolling back yourself leaves it travelling with the content. When a plugin hides the host wording and provides visible replacement text in the same content container, the theme yields its waiting line and restores it when the takeover ends; other chat animations keep running.
- **New text fades in**: Characters arriving in a streaming answer start faint and settle, staggered slightly by arrival order; a block arriving whole, a burst of thousands of characters and text that just reflowed from a fold stay solid. The Motion tab offers Fade in and Appears at once, each with a preview card, shipped on Appears at once.
- **File operation rows**: A read, edit or write of a file carries the `+n -m` tail and an expandable diff card or read window, and its path opens the file; the calls on one file inside one process group stand as a single row whose icon and title move on with the file's last operation and whose tail is their change total, turning character by character as each change lands, and opening that row reads every change — or the window it was last read at when nothing changed; a failed or interrupted row keeps its verdict.
- **Send flight**: On submission the composer card lifts and narrows into the bubble as it travels, the flying copy carrying the bubble's colour from its first frame and its words re-flowing into the shape; it meets the message row, where the real bubble shows underneath and the flying copy fades out, so the words sharpen into place.
- **Composer caret motion**: The composer's text caret is drawn by the plugin and glides when it moves; a question card's answer box and a queued message's inline editor are covered as well. The Motion tab offers On and Off, each with a preview card.
- **Chat / Trajectory tabs**: The Chat / Trajectory tab strip at the top of the conversation is redrawn as a segmented control and lifted onto the title's line when it fits.
- **Header in the title bar**: On the desktop the session title and the header's controls rise into the window's own caption row: whichever side fits is placed there — the title at the left, the controls at the right — and the rest of the title row closes up to the left.
- **Settings page**: The settings page appears both in the settings dialog (the "Claude Style" tab) and on the plugin page, in six tabs: General, Appearance, Composer, Sidebar, Conversation and Motion. Every feature that takes over part of the host's interface has its own switch; turning it off brings the host's original back at once, without a reload. A setting that only works while another one is on greys out while that one is off and says why; Header in the title bar acts on the Windows desktop window alone and greys out on every other page. Changing tabs slides the pages the way the strip reads: the new tab comes in from its side through a short blur while the one it replaces leaves to the other side; a pressed switch carries its knob past the end and back, and a page that opens shows every switch at rest. In the settings dialog the heading row ends with a link to the plugin's repository.
- **Chat / Code switch**: With [ds-bot](https://github.com/Fei2411/DeepSeek-Bot) installed, the top of the sidebar carries a Chat / Code switch like the one in Claude's desktop app: Chat is ds-bot's Bot surface, and the theme steps aside entirely, leaving only the switch and its settings page; Code is the host's own interface, painted by the theme as usual. The switch drives ds-bot's own mode change, so neither side loses what it remembers, and switching through ds-bot's own controls brings the theme along too. On the desktop it sits in the window's top row at the sidebar's right end; on the web, in the sidebar's first row.
